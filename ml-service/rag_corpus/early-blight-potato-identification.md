## Summary
Early blight makes dark brown spots on potato leaves with rings inside them, like a target. It starts on the oldest, lowest leaves and climbs the plant slowly over weeks. It never grows white fluff on the underside of the leaf. That is how you know it is not late blight.

## Body

### What the spots look like
Look at the bottom leaves first. The earliest spots are small, about 1 to 2 mm across, round or oval, and brown. As a spot grows it stops being round and turns angular, because the leaf veins block it. Inside the older spots you will see dark rings, one inside the next. Extension services call this a target spot or a bull's eye. The dead tissue is dry and papery, not slimy or soft. A yellow edge often surrounds the spot. Many spots join up, and the whole leaflet dies and hangs on the stem.

### Why the old leaves go first
This fungus attacks tissue that is already tired. Young, fast-growing leaves usually stay clean. Older leaves go first, and leaves on plants short of nitrogen go earliest of all. So the disease works up the plant from the bottom as the crop ages. This is a useful clue in itself. A leaf disease that starts low and climbs slowly over several weeks is very likely early blight.

### Four checks that separate it from late blight
1. **Rings.** Early blight spots have concentric rings inside. Late blight spots do not.
2. **White fluff.** Turn a leaf over early in the morning, while dew is on it. Late blight grows white cottony mould at the edge of the dead patch. Early blight grows nothing there.
3. **Where it starts.** Early blight starts on the old lower leaves. Late blight can start anywhere in the canopy, often at leaf tips and margins.
4. **Speed.** Early blight takes weeks. Late blight can strip a field in under two weeks. If your field is collapsing fast, treat it as late blight and report it to your Krishi Vigyan Kendra the same day.

### On tubers
Tubers can be affected too. The lesions are brown, sunken, round to irregular. Cut one open and the flesh beneath is dry, brown and corky. These lesions keep enlarging in store, and the tuber shrivels.

### What you can do without buying anything
- Rotate away from potato, tomato, brinjal and chilli for three to four years. The fungus survives on old crop debris in the soil for several years.
- After harvest, remove the haulms and bury them deep or burn them. Do not leave them heaped at the field edge.
- Pull out volunteer potato plants and solanaceous weeds.
- Do not plant potato beside a tomato or brinjal field. They host the same fungus.
- Keep nitrogen adequate and balanced. A hungry crop suffers early blight far worse.
- Space plants so the canopy dries faster after rain or dew.
- Scout the lowest leaves once a week from about 40 days after planting.

### About sprays and rates
Fungicides are used against early blight in India, and ICAR journals have published field trials on them. This document deliberately records no spray rates. A plausible but wrong rate on a food crop does more harm than no advice. For what is legally permitted on potato, and at what rate, check the CIBRC register or ask your Krishi Vigyan Kendra.
