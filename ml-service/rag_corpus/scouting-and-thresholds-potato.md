## Summary

Seeing an insect is not a reason to spray. The decision comes from counting: how
many pests, how many natural enemies, and at what crop stage. This document
explains how to walk a potato field and the few pest counts for which India has
a published action level.

## Body

### Why not spray on sight

A potato plant replaces some lost leaf area on its own. Spraying early kills the
spiders, ladybird beetles, lacewings and parasitic wasps already eating the pest.
It costs money, and repeated sprays build resistance. India's official IPM
package for potato is blunt: take no decision without first observing the field.

### How to walk the field

Start soon after the crop comes up, then once a week, in the morning before
9 a.m. Pick five spots at random. At each spot pick five plants — 25 in all. Do
not choose only the worst-looking plants. Walk a zig-zag and take what you find.

What to count on each plant:

- **Aphids, leafhoppers, whitefly, mites** — turn over five leaves. Count nymphs
  and adults.
- **Thrips** — five top leaves, or tap the leaf over paper.
- **Leaf miner** — count only the live mines on five leaves.
- **Tuber moth, cutworm, Spodoptera, Helicoverpa** — count caterpillars on the
  whole plant, and count damaged tubers.
- **Nematodes** — you cannot count these by eye. Send 200 to 300 g of root-zone
  soil for a laboratory count.

### Traps are for monitoring, not control

- Pheromone traps for tuber moth and cutworm: 4 to 5 per acre, each species at
  least 75 feet from the next, one foot above the canopy. Replace lures every 2
  to 3 weeks. Keep spare lures in a fridge.
- Yellow pan or sticky traps for whitefly, blue for thrips: 4 to 5 per acre,
  15 cm above the canopy.
- Light trap: 1 per acre, run 6 p.m. to 10 p.m.

Record the catch weekly. A rising line matters more than one number.

### Count the friends too

Also count natural enemies: ladybird beetles, lacewings, spiders, hoverflies, red
ants, reduviid bugs, and the parasitic wasps *Trichogramma*, *Bracon*,
*Apanteles* and *Encarsia*. The general rule in the Indian package is a
pest-to-defender ratio of 2:1. If defenders are keeping up, do nothing. Only when
the ratio turns against you consider action — and hand-picking, removing infested
plants and biopesticides come before any chemical.

### The action levels that are published

Most potato pests in India have no firmly fixed threshold. A few do:

- **Aphids on a seed crop:** ICAR-CPRI fixes the roving-survey critical limit at
  20 aphids per 100 compound leaves. When the count crosses it, cut the haulm.
  This is a virus-vector decision, not a sap-loss decision.
- **Virus in seed potato:** the threshold is zero. You watch the vector instead.
- **Potato cyst nematode:** ICAR-CPRI gives about 20 eggs per gram of soil, and
  notes it shifts with conditions and variety.
- **Root-knot nematode:** ICAR-CPRI refers to 10 to 20 larvae per 100 g of soil.

ICAR-CPRI also prints a monitoring table covering other potato pests, but its
columns do not line up reliably, so those pairings are not repeated here. Ask a
KVK or state agriculture officer for the local figure.

### Telling chewing damage from a disease lesion

This decides whether the answer is "pest" or "disease".

- **Look for the animal.** Turn the leaf over. Split the stem. Open the tunnel.
  An insect, a larva or dark droppings means a pest.
- **Edges.** Chewing leaves ragged holes with no coloured border. A fungal lesion
  has a defined margin, often a ring or halo.
- **Mines are tunnels, not spots.** The leaf skin stays intact and see-through.
- **Signs of the organism.** Fuzzy growth or bacterial ooze means a pathogen.
- **Pattern.** Living pathogens give irregular, patchy symptoms. Uniform, even
  symptoms across the whole field point to weather, nutrients or spray damage.
- **Below ground.** Wash the roots. Galls mean root-knot nematode. Pinhead white
  or brown bodies mean cyst nematode. Rot inside a cut stem or tuber means a
  pathogen.

Pests and diseases also arrive together. Nematode-weakened roots let in brown
rot; insect tunnels let in soft rot. Finding one does not rule out the other.

### On sprays

The Indian IPM package names chemical products and rates. None are repeated here.
Any dose must be checked against the CIBRC register first.
