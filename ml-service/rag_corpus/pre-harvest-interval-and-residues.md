## Summary
The pre-harvest interval, also called the waiting period, is the minimum number of days between your last spray and your harvest. It exists so pesticide residue on the tuber falls below the legal food limit. Digging early leaves residue on food, and it is a common reason consignments are rejected for export.

## Body

### What a pre-harvest interval is
It is a number of days, fixed for one pesticide on one crop. It runs from the last application to harvest. It is not a rule of thumb, and it does not carry across crops or products.

Rule 3(c) of the Insecticides Rules, 1971 makes it a function of the Central Insecticides Board to "advise tolerance limits for insecticides, residues and an establishment of minimum intervals between the application of insecticides and harvest in respect of various commodities".

### Why the days are needed
A pesticide breaks down over time. Sunlight, rain, soil microbes and the plant's own metabolism all reduce it. The waiting period is the time that breakdown needs.

Two things farmers often get wrong:
- A systemic product moves inside the plant and can reach the tuber. Washing does not remove it.
- Rain does not reset the clock. It may only move what sits on the leaf surface.

### The number differs for every product
On the CIB&RC Major Uses of Pesticides list, each row is one formulation on one crop for one disease, and the waiting period column changes a great deal from row to row. Two different fungicides, both registered for late blight on potato, can carry very different waiting periods. So a number from a neighbour, or from a different product, is worthless.

### How it connects to the MRL
An MRL, or maximum residue limit, is the highest amount of a pesticide residue legally allowed in a food. In India these are set by the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India.

Regulation 2.3.1 of the Food Safety and Standards (Contaminants, Toxins and Residues) Regulations, 2011 is headed "Restriction on the use of insecticides". It states that "no insecticides shall be used directly on articles of food", apart from fumigants registered for that use by the Registration Committee. It then gives a table of tolerance limits, and says the amount on the listed foods "shall not exceed the tolerance limit prescribed". Potato has its own limits in that table, separate from "other vegetables".

The waiting period is the field practice; the MRL is the test result. Follow the waiting period and the residue should fall under the MRL. Harvest early and it probably will not, and the produce is then not lawful food.

### Export rejection
Importing countries test against their own MRLs, often stricter than India's. APEDA warns exporters that compliance with the MRLs on the portals it lists "does not guarantee market access or clearance of consignments in the importing country". One over-limit consignment can put a whole Indian commodity under heavier inspection.

### Where to find the number for your own product
Use a document naming your exact formulation and crop:

- **The leaflet inside the pack.** Rule 18 requires directions for use for the approved crop.
- **The CIB&RC Major Uses of Pesticides lists** on ppqs.gov.in. The columns are Crop, Common name of the disease, Dosage per ha, and "Waiting period from last application to harvest (in days)".
- **Your Krishi Vigyan Kendra or state agriculture officer.**

Note the CIB&RC disclaimer on those lists: "The document has been compiled on the basis of available information for guidance and not for legal purposes." The approved label and certificate of registration remain the legal instruments.

If you cannot find a waiting period for your product on potato, treat that as a reason not to spray near harvest at all.

### Habits that keep you safe
- Write the date of every spray in a notebook. TNAU: "Maintain records of pesticide spraying activity."
- Plan your last spray backwards from your expected digging date.
- Never spray to "top up" just before lifting.

This document gives no day counts on purpose. The number depends on the exact product and must come from its own label.
