## Summary
The label on a pesticide pack is a legal document, not an advertisement. The Insecticides Rules, 1971 fix exactly what must be printed on it. Learn to read a few things on the label and you can tell whether a product is legal, whether it is still fresh, and how poisonous it is.

## Body

### The label is the law
Rule 16: "No person shall stock or exhibit for sale or distribute [or cause to be transported] any insecticide unless it is packed and labelled in accordance with the provisions of these rules."

Under Section 3(k) of the Insecticides Act, 1968, a pesticide is "misbranded" if its label is "false or misleading in any material particular", or if it "does not contain a warning or caution which may be necessary and sufficient". Section 18(1) forbids selling, distributing, transporting or using an insecticide that is not registered.

So the printed label is the approved use. A shopkeeper's spoken advice is not. If the two disagree, the label wins.

### The toxicity triangle
Rule 19(3) requires a square set at 45 degrees (diamond shape), "occupying not less than one-sixteenth of the total area of the face of the label". The upper triangle carries the symbol and signal word. The lower triangle carries the colour band.

Rule 19(5) fixes the colour by laboratory toxicity (LD50 by the oral route, mg/kg body weight of test animals):

| Class | Oral LD50 | Colour band | Signal word |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extremely toxic | 1-50 | Bright red | Skull and cross-bones + "POISON" in red |
| Highly toxic | 51-500 | Bright yellow | "POISON" in red |
| Moderately toxic | 501-5000 | Bright blue | "DANGER" |
| Slightly toxic | Over 5000 | Bright green | "CAUTION" |

Red, yellow and blue labels must also carry "KEEP OUT OF THE REACH OF CHILDREN". Red adds "IF SWALLOWED, OR IF, SYMPTOMS OF POISONING OCCUR CALL PHYSICIAN IMMEDIATELY".

This colour tells you how dangerous the chemical is to a person. It does not tell you how strong it is against your pest, and it is not a dose.

### What must be printed
Rule 19(1) lists eight particulars for the innermost container and the outer covering. The ones to check before you pay:

1. **Registration number.** No number means no registration.
2. **Active ingredient name and percentage.** This is the real identity. The brand name is not.
3. **Net content**, in metric units.
4. **Batch number.**
5. **Expiry date** — "up to the date the insecticide shall retain its efficiency and safety". Past it, do not use it.
6. **Antidote statement.**

The Directorate of Plant Protection tells farmers: "See Batch No., Registration Number, Date of Manufacture/ Expiry on the labels", and "Do not purchase pesticides from foot path dealers or from un-licenced person."

### No pack may claim it is safe
Rule 19(8) forbids "unwarranted claims for the safety of the producer or its ingredients", including "SAFE", "NON-POISONOUS", "NON-INJURIOUS" or "HARMLESS" — even qualified by "when used as directed". A pack making that claim is breaking the law.

### The leaflet inside the pack
Rule 18 requires a leaflet naming the plant disease, insects or weeds the product is for, directions for use, warnings, symptoms of poisoning, first-aid treatment, storage cautions, the antidote, and "instructions concerning the decontamination or safe disposal of used containers". Keep it. Do not throw it away with the carton.

### A label you can read
Rule 19(7) requires the label and leaflet to be printed "in Hindi, English and in one or two regional languages in use in the areas where the said packages are likely to be stocked, sold or distributed".

### What the label cannot do
It cannot tell you the product suits your problem. Match your crop and your named pest or disease to the label. If potato is not named for that problem, that use is not registered.
