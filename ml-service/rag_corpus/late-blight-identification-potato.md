## Summary
Late blight starts as water-soaked spots on leaf tips and margins. They turn brown, then black, within a few days. On a humid morning you can see a white, fluffy growth on the underside of the leaf, at the edge of the dead patch. That white growth is the most reliable sign, and it is what separates late blight from early blight.

## Body

### The first spots
Look for pale green, water-soaked spots, about 2 to 10 mm across. They appear most often on leaf tips and leaf margins. In wet weather they enlarge quickly, turn black, and kill the whole leaf. A lighter green or yellow halo often surrounds the dead area. TNAU records the sequence as spots that "increase in size, turn purple brown& finally black colour."

### The white growth underneath
Turn the leaf over early in the morning, while dew is still on it. Look at the edge of the dead patch, not the middle. Late blight produces a whitish, cottony growth there. TNAU states plainly: "White growth develops on under surface of leaves." AHDB lists two key identification points for leaf blight: browning of veins on the lower leaf surface, and white, fluffy pathogen growth at the advancing edge of the lesion. It is clearest in warm, humid conditions. It fades once the leaf dries, so check early in the day.

### Stems and tubers
Light brown lesions form on stems and petioles. They lengthen, ring the stem, and the plant collapses at that point. On tubers, the skin shows sunken, firm, purplish-brown patches. Cut the tuber open. Under the patch the flesh is rusty brown and granular, spreading inward from the surface. Soft rot bacteria often follow and turn it wet and smelly.

### How fast it moves
Very fast. The latent period is the gap between infection and new spores forming on the leaf. AHDB reports it as about 4.5 to 5.5 days at 13 °C, and only 3 to 4 days at 18 °C, for most pathogen genotypes tested. So one cycle is under a week. A field can go from a few spots to complete loss of foliage in under two weeks. A delay of a few days decides whether you keep the crop.

### Early blight looks different
Early blight is a different disease, caused by the fungus *Alternaria solani*. It is far less urgent. Four checks separate them:

- Early blight spots are brown-black and angular or oval, with clear concentric rings inside, like a target. Late blight spots have no rings.
- Early blight has no white fluffy growth on the leaf underside.
- Early blight usually starts on the older, lower leaves. Late blight often starts at leaf tips and margins anywhere in the canopy.
- Early blight is favoured by dry warm weather with intermittent rain, around 25 to 30 °C. Late blight needs cool and wet weather.

### If you are unsure
Photograph a fresh, spreading lesion, with the leaf turned over, in the morning. Photograph the whole leaf, not only the dead centre. Report a suspected late blight outbreak to your nearest Krishi Vigyan Kendra or state agriculture officer the same day. Speed matters more than certainty here.
