How people move through it

Ruled out

A bottom tab bar

The obvious mobile answer, and it does not survive our own constraints. Four apply at once:

48–64 px targets
200% text zoom must not clip
360 px screen floor
~2× string expansion in Telugu

Five Telugu labels at 200% text inside 360 px, each in a 48 px-wide tab, is not a tight fit — it is arithmetic that does not close.

Chosen

A flat list of large rows

Microsoft Research compared hierarchical menus against flat lists with exactly this population — emergent users, low literacy. Flat wins. It is also the only layout that fits a long Telugu label at double size, so the research and the arithmetic agree.

Each row carries a label, an icon, and recorded audio — a human voice per language, not text-to-speech. A row readable three ways is a row that works for someone who cannot read it.

The circle on the right plays the row name aloud. Tapping the row opens it.

Rows are 72 px tall, clearing the 64 px target floor with room for a second line when a translated label wraps. The icon sits in a bordered square rather than floating: the high-contrast rule forbids drop shadows, because a soft edge is the first thing to vanish in sunlight.

Nav depth is capped at two. Any farmer task is reachable in at most two taps from this list. If a flow needs three, the flow is wrong, not the menu.

Deliberately generic

The farmer flow should not look inventive. The camera should feel like WhatsApp’s camera; the money list like a UPI transaction history. Those are the two interfaces this user already knows, and familiarity beats originality when the cost of confusion is a missed harvest payment.

Anti-slop discipline applies to the investor, buyer and marketing surfaces, where someone is comparing and judging and a distinct voice earns attention. Two audiences, two standards, one token set.
