## Summary
The Central Insecticides Board and Registration Committee, or CIB&RC, keeps India's legal register of pesticides. It records which products are registered, which crops and pests each is registered for, and which are banned or restricted. The lists are free and public on ppqs.gov.in, and you can check any product yourself.

## Body

### What CIB&RC is
The Insecticides Act, 1968 created two bodies. The Central Insecticides Board advises the government. The Registration Committee registers products. The Directorate explains the split: "there is compulsory registration of the pesticides at the Central level and licence for their manufacture, formulation and sale are dealt with at the State level."

Section 9(3) says the Committee registers a product only "after satisfying itself that the insecticide to which the application relates conforms to the claims made by the importer or by the manufacturer", and must then "allot a registration number thereto and issue a certificate of registration". That number ties the pack in the shop back to a government decision.

Section 18(1) forbids any person to sell, stock, distribute, transport or use any insecticide "which is not registered under this Act", or any insecticide "in contravention of any other provision of this Act or of any rule made thereunder".

Registration covers a product **and** specific uses. A fungicide registered for tomato is not registered for potato. Using it on potato falls outside the conditions of registration.

### How to look a product up
Start at **https://ppqs.gov.in**. In the top menu open **Divisions**, then **Central Insecticides Board & Registration Committee**. That submenu holds the pages below. (The site was migrated in 2026; if a label has moved, search the site for the document title.)

**"Is this product registered at all?"** Open **Registered Products**:
- "List of Insecticides / Pesticides Registered under section 9(3) of the Insecticides Act, 1968 for use in the Country as on 31.03.2026" — https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/list_of_pesticides_as_on_31.03.2026.pdf
- "LIST OF PESTICIDES FORMULATIONS REGISTERED FOR USE IN THE COUNTRY UNDER THE INSECTICIDES ACT, 1968 AS ON 31.03.2026" — https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/list_pf_pesticide_formulations_registered_as_on_31.03.2026.pdf

**"Is it legal on potato, for my pest?"** Open **Major Uses of Pesticides**. Separate lists cover Insecticides, Fungicides, Bio-Pesticides (biofungicide), Herbicides, Plant Growth Regulators and Bio-Insecticides. Each row gives Crop, Common name of the disease, Dosage per ha, and "Waiting period from last application to harvest (in days)". Open the list for your product type and search for your crop. The fungicide list, which covers late blight on potato, is at https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/2._chemical_mup_fungicide_as_on_31.03.2026_0.pdf

**"Is it banned or restricted?"** Also under Registered Products: "LIST OF PESTICIDES WHICH ARE BANNED, REFUSED REGISTRATION AND RESTRICTED IN USE AS ON 31.07.2026" — https://ppqs.gov.in/sites/default/files/list_of_pesticides_which_are_banned_refused_registration_and_restricted_in_use.pdf

That one file holds three sections, and the difference matters:
- **I. Pesticides / formulations banned in India**, with sub-lists. Some are "banned for manufacture, import and use". Others are banned for use but still manufactured for export — which is exactly why a product banned for your field can still exist in India. Others are withdrawn.
- **II. Pesticides refused registration.**
- **III. Pesticides restricted for use in the country.** Restricted means permitted only in particular ways, for particular crops, or by particular handlers. One entry, for example, bans a chemical as a foliar spray while allowing another use.

Each entry names the gazette notification (an "S.O." number and date) behind the decision. A product being offered to you is not proof it is permitted.

### Search on the chemical, not the brand
The register lists active ingredients by common name, with the percentage and a formulation code. Shops sell brand names. So read the active ingredient, the percentage and the registration number off the pack, then search the PDF for that active ingredient.

### Read the disclaimer on the Major Uses list
Those lists carry CIB&RC's own note: "The document has been compiled on the basis of available information for guidance and not for legal purposes." Treat them as a lookup tool. The approved label and the certificate of registration are the legal instruments. Where list and label disagree, follow the label and ask an extension officer.

### One row is one row
Every number belongs to one exact combination: one formulation, one crop, one pest. Do not carry a figure across crops, across pests, or to a different percentage of the same chemical. That is how wrong doses spread.

### When the answer is no
If your crop and pest pair does not appear, or the chemical sits on the banned or restricted list, do not use the product. Ask your Krishi Vigyan Kendra or state agriculture department for a registered option, and ask them to show you the row.
