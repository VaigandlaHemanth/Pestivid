## Summary
Most of India's potato is a winter crop in the northern plains, planted around October and lifted by March. A much smaller area is a summer crop in the hills, and the Nilgiris grows more than one crop a year. The same spot on a leaf can mean different diseases in these different seasons, so the planting date and the district matter as much as the photograph.

## Body

### Where the crop is grown
Scientists at ICAR-Central Potato Research Institute divide India into eight potato zones: "the Northwestern plains (Punjab, Haryana and Rajasthan); (ii) the West-central plains (Uttar Pradesh and northwestern districts of Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh), (iii) North-eastern plains (Assam, Bihar, Jharkhand, West Bengal, Orissa, eastern Uttar Pradesh...), (iv) the plateau region (central and peninsular India...); (v) the Northwestern hills (Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttaranchal); (vi) the Northeastern hills...; (vii) the Southern hills (Nilgiris and Palini hills of Tamil Nadu), and (viii) the Sikkim and West Bengal hills".

### When each region plants
The same paper states that "Nearly 85-90% of potato is grown in the vast Indo-Gangetic plains of north India during the winter season (Rabi) from October to March, while <5% of the area is cultivated in the Hills during the summer season from April to September/October." Plateau regions account for about 6% of the area.

The crop needs a window of at least 70 days with a maximum temperature below 35 degrees C and a minimum between 2 and 21 degrees C. That is why the plains crop must start and finish inside the cool winter.

For Tamil Nadu, TNAU gives three plantings in the hills: "Summer (March-April), Autumn (August-September), Irrigated (January-February)". For the plains it says planting "is done during October - November".

### Why the season changes the answer
Late blight needs cool, wet air. The cited paper's literature review gives a favourable band of 15 to 28 degrees C maximum temperature with 90 to 100% relative humidity. Its own field observations of disease initiation were a maximum of 30.7 C and morning RH of 96.7%.

- **Rabi, northern plains.** Blight risk peaks in the cool, foggy weather of late December and January. But the same weeks bring frost. Blackened leaves in a plains crop in January may be blight, or may be frost. Check the leaf underside for white growth before deciding.
- **Kharif, hills.** The crop grows through the monsoon, so leaves stay wet for long spells and blight pressure lasts most of the season.
- **Southern hills (Nilgiris).** Three plantings a year mean there is never a clean break. Living potato is almost always present, so blight inoculum and soil problems such as potato cyst nematode carry over from crop to crop.
- **Plateau kharif.** Warm and wet. Bacterial soft rot suits these conditions; TNAU records it developing best at 21 to 29 degrees C with about 94% relative humidity. Cool-weather blight is less likely than in a plains rabi crop.

### What to ask before answering
Ask the district and the planting date. An October-planted plains crop showing spots in November is in warm weather, so early blight or a bacterial problem is more likely than late blight. The same photograph from the same field in January points the other way.
