## Summary

Aphids and whitefly suck sap, but the sap loss is rarely the real problem. The
real problem is that they carry potato viruses from plant to plant. There is no
cure for a virus once a plant has it. So controlling the insect is how you
control the virus.

## Body

### Why the vector link matters

A virus cannot walk. It needs an insect to carry it into a healthy plant. The
insect feeds on the phloem of a sick plant, picks up the virus, then feeds on a
healthy plant and passes it on. ICAR-CPRI reports that potato viruses cause
losses of 20 to 50% under severe infection.

So a farmer who sees curled or mottled leaves should also look for the insect.
Spraying a fungicide on a virus does nothing. Removing the sick plants and
reducing the insect does.

### Which insects, which viruses

Three aphids matter on potato in India: green peach aphid (*Myzus persicae*),
cotton aphid (*Aphis gossypii*) and potato aphid (*Macrosiphum euphorbiae*).
Green peach aphid is the most efficient carrier.

Aphids carry PVY, PVA, PVM and PVS in a non-persistent way: the aphid picks the
virus up in seconds and loses it quickly, so a short probe by a passing aphid is
enough. Aphids carry PLRV (potato leaf roll virus) persistently — a longer feed
is needed, but the aphid then stays infective.

Whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*) carries Tomato leaf curl New Delhi virus, which
causes potato apical leaf curl disease. Thrips (*Thrips palmi*) carry stem
necrosis virus.

The practical difference: because non-persistent viruses spread in seconds,
killing aphids after they arrive is often too late. Keeping numbers low from the
start, and cutting the haulm early, work better.

### Direct damage

Aphids suck sap and leaves turn pale and dry. Whitefly nymphs and adults suck sap
from the leaf underside; leaves yellow and curl. Honeydew from both feeds a black
sooty mould that coats the leaf and blocks light.

### How to recognise them

Aphids are soft, small, mostly green, and sit still in colonies on the underside
of leaves. Green peach aphid adults are 1.2 to 2.1 mm. Whiteflies are about 1 mm,
covered in white waxy powder, and fly up in a cloud when you shake the leaf.
Whitefly numbers build fast — ICAR-CPRI reports 11 to 15 generations a year.

### Management that works

**Time the crop to miss the vector.** The single most effective step for seed
potato. ICAR-CPRI advises planting up to 15 October in the north-western plains,
up to 25 October in the central plains and up to 5 November in the north-eastern
plains, so the crop grows in the low-aphid period.

**Cut the haulm on the aphid count.** ICAR-CPRI advises dehaulming a seed crop as
soon as aphids cross the critical level of 20 per 100 compound leaves. Stop the
stumps re-sprouting before harvest.

**Trap and monitor.** Yellow water pan or yellow sticky traps, 60 per hectare,
for aphids. For whitefly, 15 by 30 cm yellow sticky traps just above the canopy,
60 per hectare, evenly spaced.

**Remove the reservoirs.** Take out weeds, volunteer potato plants and nearby
host plants like okra, brinjal and datura.

**Use clean seed.** Plant certified, disease-free seed. A virus in the seed tuber
starts the epidemic inside your own field.

**Rogue infected plants.** Pull out and destroy plants showing virus symptoms.

**Trap crop for whitefly.** Cucumber is a favoured whitefly host but not a host
for the virus. Whiteflies lose their ability to transmit after feeding on it a
few days.

**Variety choice.** ICAR-CPRI notes potato cv. Kufri Bahar is highly tolerant and
can be planted in whitefly-prone areas.

**Tool hygiene.** PVX, PVS and the spindle tuber viroid spread on hands, clothes
and tools, not by insects. Disinfect field equipment.

### When pest damage looks like a disease

Sap-sucking damage and virus symptoms are easy to confuse, and they often occur
together. Yellowing and curling from whitefly feeding looks like the leaf curl
virus the whitefly carries. Sooty mould looks like a fungal disease but grows on
honeydew and does not attack the plant.

The test is to turn the leaf over and look for the insect. If insects are present
in numbers, the cause is a pest. If leaves are mottled, rolled or stunted with no
insects on them, suspect a virus already in the plant or the seed.

### A note on sprays

ICAR-CPRI names insecticides and seed treatments for vector control. No rate is
repeated here. Any dose must be checked against the CIBRC register first.
