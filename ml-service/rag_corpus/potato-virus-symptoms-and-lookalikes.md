## Summary

Potato viruses make leaves mottled, rolled, pale or curled. Low nitrogen, dry soil
and heat cause the same signs. Guessing wrong is expensive, because a virus cannot
be cured and a fungicide will not touch it.

## Body

### The viruses found on potato in India

ICAR-CPRI names seven common potato viruses in India: PVX, PVS, PVY, PVA, PVM, PLRV
and PALCV, plus the viroid PSTVd.

Aphids carry PVY, PVA, PVM and PVS non-persistently, and PLRV persistently.
Whitefly carries apical leaf curl. PVX, PVS and PSTVd need no insect at all — they
move on hands, clothes, cutting knives and spray equipment.

### What each looks like on the leaf

**PVY.** ICAR-CPRI describes "Mottling of leaves with light to dark green patches
interspersed with normal green colour and severe mosaic", with necrotic spots,
veinal necrosis, leaf drop, and "Rugosity, twisting of leaves with slight
inter-veinal cuppings".

**PVX.** "Mild to moderately severe perceptible mottling of leaves with light to
dark green patches interspersed with normal green colour." ICAR-CPRI adds it is
"Latent in many varieties" — the plant carries it and looks fine.

**PVA.** "Mosaic faint mottling, sometimes leaf distortions, top necrosis in some
varieties, rarely rugosity, shiny leaves."

**PLRV (leaf roll).** Two different pictures. Infected this season by aphids: top
young leaves stand upright, roll, go slightly pale, and most varieties redden or
pink at the top leaf margins. Grown from an infected seed tuber: the older lower
leaves show it, as a "pale, dwarfed, and upright appearance with rolling of lower
leaves that turn yellow, brittle and are leathery in texture". Pressed between the
fingers those leaves "give rustling sound". Yield loss in India normally runs 20 to
50 per cent, in extreme cases 50 to 80 per cent.

**Apical leaf curl.** A strain of Tomato leaf curl New Delhi virus. Leaves show
"chlorosis, crinckling, mosaic, curling of apical leaves, pinkish tinge on leaf
margins", and the plant later looks bushy and stunted as internodes shorten.
ICAR-CPRI reports 40 to 70 per cent infection in the Indo-Gangetic plains.

### Why these are easy to get wrong

**Symptoms come and go with the weather.** ICAR-CPRI notes apical leaf curl
symptoms "are recovered as the maximum temperature fall below 28°C". UC IPM reports
PVY mottling is masked at low and at high temperatures. A field can look sick one
week and normal the next.

**Hunger looks like this.** Pale stunted plants with yellowing lower leaves also
describe nitrogen shortage. Reddish, purple leaf margins also describe phosphorus
shortage.

**Dry soil looks like this.** Leaf margins roll inward in hot dry weather on
perfectly healthy plants.

**Many viruses hide.** PVS is "Usually latent"; PVX is latent in many varieties.

**Viruses look like each other.** ICAR-CPRI notes that at low concentration apical
leaf curl produces rolling "just like symptoms caused by Potato virus M". UC IPM
states the virus usually cannot be identified from symptoms alone.

### Checks that help in the field

- **Pattern.** Nutrient shortage and drought affect a patch evenly, or follow slope
  and soil. Virus starts as scattered single plants, often worst at the field edge.
- **Feel.** A rolled leaf that is leathery, brittle and rustles points to leaf roll
  virus. Soft and limp points to water stress.
- **Irrigate and look again.** Rolling from dry soil relaxes after watering. Rolling
  from virus does not.
- **Turn the leaf over.** Aphids or whitefly in numbers mean virus may be spreading
  now.
- **Confirm in a lab for a seed crop.** ICAR-CPRI indexes seed tubers by ELISA
  against PVX, PVS, PVM, PVA, PVY and PLRV. Cornell notes PLRV is hard to detect
  because foliar symptoms are not always obvious.

### Do not buy a fungicide for this

A fungicide kills fungi. A virus is not a fungus. Confirm what the problem is
before spending.
