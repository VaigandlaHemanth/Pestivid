## Summary
Most pesticide exposure happens through the skin, and mostly on the hands while mixing. Gloves, boots and covered skin are the protection that matters most, and they cost little. If someone is exposed, wash them, take the pack to the doctor, and call the poison centre.

## Body

### Where the poison gets in
Damalas and Koutroubas (*Toxics*, 2016) report that "the dermal and inhalation routes of entry are typically the most common routes of farmers' exposure to pesticides", and that hands and forearms take the greatest contamination while mixing and spraying. In one study they cite, dermal exposure fell 65 per cent when gloves and coveralls were used together. So mixing is the dangerous stage: the chemical is concentrated and hands touch it.

### What is realistic, and what is non-negotiable
Rule 39(4) of the Insecticides Rules, 1971 defines a full suit: overalls, gloves "extending half-way up to the fore-arm, made of materials impermeable to liquids", "dust-proof goggles" and "boots". That is often out of reach. This priority order is not:

1. **Impermeable gloves to the forearm.** TNAU: "Do not mix pesticides in bare hands." The worst habit.
2. **Boots or closed shoes.** Never bare feet in a sprayed or wet field.
3. **Full-sleeve shirt and trousers**, kept only for spraying, washed after each use and apart from family clothes.
4. **Eye protection while mixing.** Concentrate splashes upward.
5. **Cloth over nose and mouth.** Better than nothing for dust and droplets, but not a respirator.

For a red-label or yellow-label product, gloves and eye protection are not optional: "Never prepare spray solution without wearing protective clothings."

### Mixing safely
- Use clean water, not muddy or stagnant. Mix away from wells, ponds and canals.
- Use a dedicated jug and stick. Mixing vessels "should never be used for domestic purpose even after thorough washing".
- "Never transfer pesticides from original to another containers." Never a drinking-water bottle.
- "Do not mix incompatible pesticides or pesticides with fertilizers or micronutrients."
- Use the mix immediately; do not keep it beyond 24 hours.
- "Do not blow/clean clogged nozzles with mouth. Instead use tooth brush tied with sprayer."
- "Do not eat, drink, smoke or chew during whole operation of pesticides."

### Spray drift
- "Do not spray in high wind, high temperature and rain."
- "Do not spray pesticides against wind." Spray "in the wind direction".
- "Start spraying near the down wind edge of the field and proceed upwind so that operator moves into unsprayed area."
- "Never spray if the wind is blowing towards grazing livestock or pastures regularly used."
- Keep children and bystanders away.

### After spraying, and empty containers
Remove and wash protective clothing and footwear, wash yourself, and change clothes before eating or smoking. Dispose of leftover solution "at safer place viz. barren isolated area", never into canals or ponds. Rule 44 requires that used packages "shall not be left outside to prevent their re-use" and "shall be broken and buried away from habitation". Never reuse a container for water, oil, feed or grain, and never as a scoop. For a spill, "use sawdust or sand and decontaminate with lime."

### First aid for exposure
- Move the person to fresh air. Remove contaminated clothing at once. Wash skin with soap and plenty of water. Rinse eyes with clean running water.
- Do not make the person vomit unless the label says to.
- **Take the pack and leaflet to the doctor:** "On observing poisoning symptoms give the first aid and show the patient to doctor. Also show the empty container to doctor." The label carries the antidote statement (Rule 19(1)); the leaflet carries symptoms and first aid (Rule 18). That is what lets a doctor treat correctly.
- Rule 38: "In all cases of poisoning first-aid treatment shall always be given before the physician is called."
- **National Poisons Information Centre, AIIMS New Delhi: toll free 1800 116 117**, "24 hrs a day, 7 days a week". Its advice: "Do not wait for the victim to look or feel sick. Do not treat the person yourself. Take the victim to near by healthcare facility."
