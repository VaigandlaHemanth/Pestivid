## Summary
Almost every Indian potato variety was bred by ICAR-Central Potato Research Institute (ICAR-CPRI), Shimla. CPRI publishes a late blight rating for each one. The ratings run from highly resistant down to susceptible. Choosing a resistant variety is the cheapest protection you can buy, but no Indian variety is immune, and a rating can go out of date.

## Body

### How to read CPRI's ratings
CPRI Technical Bulletin No. 78 gives each variety a line reading "Late blight - highly resistant", "resistant", "field resistant", "moderately resistant" or "susceptible". Resistant does not mean immune. It means the disease grows more slowly, so you have more days to act.

|---|---|---|---|
| Kufri Girdhari | 2008 | highly resistant | Indian hills |

| Kufri Neelima | 2010 | resistant | Nilgiri hills |
| Kufri Swarna | 1985 | resistant | South Indian hills |
| Kufri Megha | 1989 | resistant | North-eastern hills |
| Kufri Sherpa | 1983 | resistant, incl. tubers | North Bengal hills, Sikkim |
| Kufri Himalini | 2006 | moderately resistant | North Indian hills |
| Kufri Giriraj | 1998 | moderately resistant | North Indian hills |
| Kufri Shailja | 2005 | moderately resistant | hills |
| Kufri Sahyadri | 2019 | moderately resistant | Nilgiri hills |

Some of these also resist potato cyst nematode, which matters in the Nilgiris. CPRI rates Kufri Neelima and Kufri Swarna "highly resistant" to cyst nematodes. ICAR records Kufri Karan as "highly resistant to late blight, resistant to six potato viruses and potato cyst nematodes (PCN)."

### Widely grown varieties that are not protected
This is the more useful half of the list. CPRI rates Kufri Bahar, Kufri Chandramukhi, Kufri Ashoka, Kufri Sindhuri, Kufri Lauvkar and Kufri Surya as susceptible. Kufri Pukhraj, the most widely planted variety in the north Indian plains, is only moderately resistant. If you grow these, you depend entirely on scouting and on acting in time.

### A newer release for the hills
ICAR-CPRI's Kufri Abhedya has been notified by the Government of India. The press report cited here does not give the notification year, so treat the date as unconfirmed; seed is not yet widely available. Press reporting describes it as combining "high productivity with resistance to late blight and potato cyst nematodes," recommended for Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh, the north-eastern hills, the Nilgiris, and the plateau of Karnataka and Maharashtra. Seed will take a few seasons to reach farmers. Ask your Krishi Vigyan Kendra or state seed agency.

### Resistance wears out - this is important
Do not treat an old rating as a promise. A screening study at ICAR-CPRI, Shimla found Kufri Girdhari highly resistant but Kufri Jyoti highly susceptible, though CPRI's bulletin lists Kufri Jyoti as moderately resistant. The same study reported that the R1 resistance gene no longer provides late blight resistance.

The pathogen changed. A review of *Phytophthora infestans* in Asia records that the aggressive, metalaxyl-resistant EU_13_A2 lineage was reported in India by 2008. AHDB states the general rule: "The resistance of a variety is not necessarily durable."

So use a resistant variety, but keep scouting. Resistance buys days. It does not replace looking at your crop.
