## Summary

Potato tuber moth is a caterpillar pest. It mines potato leaves and stems in
the field, but the worst damage is to tubers in storage, where losses of up to
100% have been reported in India. It is a problem in the hills and the plateau,
not usually in the Indo-Gangetic plains.

## Body

### What it is

Potato tuber moth (PTM), *Phthorimaea operculella*, is a small moth. It came to
India in 1906 in seed potato imported from Italy. Heavy infestation is reported
from Himachal Pradesh, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, the north-eastern hill states and
the plateau region. It is mostly absent from the Indo-Gangetic plains because of
the hot summer and because almost all potato there goes into modern cold stores.

### Damage in the field

The caterpillar is the damaging stage, not the moth. Young caterpillars mine
inside the leaf, leaving the upper and lower leaf skin intact. The mined area
turns see-through but looks brownish from the droppings inside. The caterpillar
may then move into the leaf stalk and on into the main stem. When it works inside
the stem, the top of the plant often dies.

Caterpillars reach tubers by travelling down cracks in the soil, not by boring
through the stem. Exposed or shallow tubers are at highest risk. Damage is
usually worst at the field edges facing the wind.

### Why storage is worse

In the field the moth prefers to lay on green foliage. Once the haulm dies back,
the pest moves to the tubers. So the longer tubers sit in the field after haulm
cutting, the more they get infested. In store, a tuber pile is a concentrated
food supply with no rain, no predators and no dry season to break the cycle.
Tunnels fill with droppings and fungi, and rot follows the tunnels in.

ICAR-CPRI states that potatoes should be stored between 7.2 and 10 degrees C,
and that because the moth does not develop below 5 degrees C it causes no
economic problem in cold storage. The severe losses happen in country stores,
sheds and heaps under trees.

### Pheromone traps

Traps catch male moths using the female scent. They tell you the pest has
arrived and how fast numbers are rising. They are for monitoring, not control.
ICAR-CPRI recommends one pheromone capsule per trap, in delta-shaped sticky
traps or hooded water pan traps, at 10 to 12 traps per hectare in the field and
4 traps per 100 cubic metres in storage. TNAU recommends 15 per hectare in the
field. Put field traps on the margins. Change the sticky liner or the water
weekly and the lure monthly.

### Non-chemical management

- Plant healthy, pest-free seed tubers.
- Plant deeper. ICAR-CPRI reports mean tuber infestation of 9% at 10 cm planting
  depth against 18% at 6 cm, and that deeper planting with timely earthing up
  cuts infestation by up to 50%.
- Keep more than 5 cm of soil over the tubers all season. Earth up in time.
- Keep the soil moist late in the season. Moths prefer dry soil for egg laying,
  and irrigation closes the cracks the caterpillars use.
- Harvest soon after haulm cutting. Never leave harvested tubers in the field
  overnight.
- Destroy cull piles and volunteer plants. They carry the pest to next season.
- Intercrop with chilli, onion or pea.
- In country stores, cover the heap with dried lantana leaves above and below.
  ICAR-CPRI reports this reduces damage by 90%. Eucalyptus and eupatorium leaves
  also work, staying effective about six months in Shillong conditions.
- Remove damaged tubers before storing.

### When pest damage looks like a disease

A tuber moth leaf mine can look like a fungal leaf spot or early blight. Three
things separate them. A mine is a tunnel, not a lesion, and dark droppings are
often packed inside it. The leaf skin stays intact over the mine. And if you tear
the mine open you find a small caterpillar.

Tuber tunnels are round holes filled with frass, not the soft sunken rot of a
bacterial disease — though rot often follows later. A dead plant top caused by a
stem-boring caterpillar can be mistaken for wilt, so split the stem and look for
the tunnel.

### A note on sprays

ICAR-CPRI and TNAU both name insecticides for this pest. No rate is repeated
here. Any dose must be checked against the CIBRC register first.
